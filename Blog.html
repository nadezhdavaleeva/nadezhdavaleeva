Nadezhda Valeeva

CV Contact Email

Nadezhda Valeeva is a cultural studies researcher working at the intersection of media theory, philosophy of technology, and the study of educational and institutional cultures.

Their research treats translation, mediation, and experimentation as practices through which knowledge and culture are made to circulate, with attention to neural machine translation, the politics of nostalgia and commemoration in educational spaces, and philosophical thought on technology, from Kant to Dewey.

They are pursuing an MA in Digital and Public Humanities at Ca’ Foscari University of Venice, following a BA in Cultural Studies with a minor in Film and Media Studies at the School of Advanced Studies, University of Tyumen (2022–2026), completed summa cum laude. Their thesis, “Forms of Reduction in Neural Machine Translation Technology,” was supervised by Prof. Devin Wangert and reviewed by Dr. Jacob Gaboury.

In 2025, they were a junior researcher at Skolkovo, working on commemorative practices in educational spaces, and a research intern in the Mediation Department of the V-A-C Foundation’s GES-2 House of Culture in Moscow.

Recent work includes “Perpetual Experiment: Dewey and the Politics of Experimentation in Education” (2025), Ecce Homo, and an essay on Kant’s philosophical anthropology (2025), Immanuel Kant Baltic Federal University Press.

They have been a teaching assistant at the School of Advanced Studies, University of Tyumen, for courses in writing, ethics, and philosophy, and for the urban studies course City as Text.

They have presented at conferences in Moscow and Saint Petersburg, including the “Vectors” International Conference of Young Scientists, the GES-2 forum “Radical Stability,” and the student conference “Philosophy as Tradition and Value.”

They hold the Ca’ Foscari Merit-Based Scholarship for International Students and a Fondazione Alfonso Coin scholarship, and were a prizewinner at the II International Philosophy Olympiad for Students (2024).

They headed the SAS Undergraduate Research Community in 2024–25 and 2025–26.
